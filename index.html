03 — REFLECTIONS
01 / GENERAL EDUCATION

Seeing the bigger picture

Engineering decisions have economic, global, and societal consequences. My general education coursework has helped me think about who will use a solution, what resources they have, and how design choices affect them.

Read reflection +

Spanish coursework, including SPAN 201P, helped me consider communication across languages and the needs of people with different backgrounds. RUS 375 encouraged me to consider historical and societal context when thinking about the consequences of decisions.

These experiences remind me to look beyond technical performance. A useful engineering solution also needs to be affordable, safe, reliable, and appropriate for the resources and infrastructure available to its users.

02 / CUMULATIVE REFLECTION

Learning how to learn

My education has helped me approach unfamiliar problems with more confidence: identify the known information, break the problem into smaller steps, and test whether the result makes sense.

Read reflection +

Laboratory projects taught me that a design must be tested, not just calculated. On the temperature indicator project, adjusting resistor values and checking the thresholds helped connect circuit theory with practical behavior.

My learning strategies also changed. When lectures alone were not enough, I used additional explanations, worked with classmates, and formed study groups. Collaboration helped me compare approaches and communicate my reasoning.

If I were starting again, I would pursue internships earlier and build stronger relationships with professors and classmates. As I begin my career, I want to apply what I have learned to real electrical systems and continue developing through mentorship and independent learning.
